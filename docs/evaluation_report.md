## 1. Audit of Phases 0-3 (before testing)

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Offline test suite | 202 passed (before Phase 4), 211 passed after (9 new evaluation tests) |
| Database, Ollama | Docker PostgreSQL healthy; `qwen2.5:7b` available |
| Corpus | 377 papers, 381 source records, 148 PDFs, 7,691 chunks |
| Phase 3 test questions | 15/15 passed earlier today (`docs/rag_eval_results.md`); those rules test safety, not quality |

Problems found by the audit (details and fixes in section 7):

1. **The HNSW vector index is never used by the retrieval query.** With
   `hnsw.ef_search = 200` PostgreSQL estimates the index scan as more expensive
   than a sequential scan of 7,691 rows, so every search is an exact scan
   (`EXPLAIN ANALYZE`: `Seq Scan on paper_chunks` + `top-N heapsort`). The
   comment in `settings.yaml` that ef_search=200 "matches exact search" is true
   for the wrong reason: it *is* exact search.
2. **The corpus covers four days only.** All 377 papers have year 2026; only
   `daily` runs were executed (2026-10-30 to 2026-11-02), `backfill` never ran.
3. **About a quarter of the corpus is off-topic** (labour markets, construction
   supply chains, Ukrainian finance papers) - the OpenAlex query for
   `ai_for_cybersecurity` matches any paper mentioning AI and cybersecurity.
4. **Title normalisation drops non-Latin titles.** `normalize_title` keeps only
   `[a-z0-9]`, so 6 Cyrillic titles get `normalized_title = NULL` and can never
   be matched by the title+year duplicate rule.
5. **Same-title duplicates remain.** 4 pairs of Zenodo re-deposits (same title,
   same first author, different DOIs) are stored twice, because two different
   DOIs are treated as proof of two different papers.
6. **Reference lists leak into chunks.** 96 chunks (1.2%) look like
   bibliographies (heuristic: >= 4 `[n]` markers and >= 4 years), where the
   "References" heading was not detected.

## 2. Evaluation dataset

File: `config/eval/golden_set.yaml` (40 questions).

- **30 answerable questions** across the three topics (intrusion detection,
  network security, AI for security), incl. one Vietnamese question that shares
  its relevance labels with its English twin (`federated-ids`).
- **10 unanswerable questions**: 4 off-domain (cake, football, Riemann,
  ibuprofen) and 6 *hard* in-domain ones (NotPetya at Maersk, Log4Shell CVE,
  Kerberos TGT lifetime, Stuxnet PLCs, BGP hijack detection, WPA3 Dragonblood).
  A full-text check found no chunk that answers them (Stuxnet/NotPetya/Log4j
  are only *mentioned* in passing in 2 papers).
- **Relevance judgments (pooling).** For each answerable question the pool was
  the top-15 papers from the system's dense retrieval + the top-10 papers from
  PostgreSQL full-text search over title+abstract (an independent lexical
  ranker), + a manual scan of all titles (`scripts/build_judgment_pool.py`). A
  paper is relevant when its title/abstract shows it directly addresses the
  question. 101 relevant judgments in total (1-11 per question).
- **Who judged:** the AI assistant (Claude), from titles and abstracts, not a
  domain expert. These are *silver* labels; a human review of
  `golden_set.yaml` would make them gold. Because half of each pool comes from
  the system under test, recall is measured against "relevant papers that at
  least one of two rankers found", which overestimates true recall.
- **Key points** (for completeness): 2-4 any-of word groups per question taken
  from the relevant abstracts.

## 4. Retrieval (measured)

Settings: `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, top_k = 8 chunks sent to the LLM, candidate_k =
40, max 3 chunks per paper. Ranking is evaluated at paper level (distinct
papers in retrieval order). 29 English answerable questions; the Vietnamese
question is reported separately because retrieval alone does not translate.
Relevant papers without any indexed text (15 of 101 judgments) are excluded
from the recall denominator and reported as a data-pipeline loss.

| Metric | @1 | @3 | @5 | @10 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Precision@K | 0.793 | 0.483 | 0.352 | 0.200 |
| Recall@K | 0.433 | 0.686 | 0.756 | 0.821 |
| Hit@K (>= 1 relevant paper) | 0.793 | 0.897 | 0.931 | 1.000 |

- **MRR 0.858.**
- **Relevant evidence coverage:** the 8 chunks sent to the LLM contain at least
  one relevant paper for **93.1%** (27/29) of the questions; on average they
  cover **75.2%** of the indexed relevant papers, and **52.2%** of the 8 chunks
  come from relevant papers.
- Precision@5/10 are capped by the number of relevant papers: 9 questions
  have only 1 relevant paper, so their P@5 cannot exceed 0.2.
- **Retrieval latency** (query embedding + database search, Python side):
  mean 49 ms, p95 58 ms (40 questions).
- **Failures:** `federated-ids` (MRR 0.17) and `ids-datasets` (MRR 0.14). For
  both, a 500-page management book (`doi:10.66414/mls.978-1-0666009-1-5`) and
  generic surveys rank first: long generic documents produce many chunks that
  are close to any security question ("hub" chunks).
- **Vietnamese without translation:** the raw Vietnamese question reaches best
  similarity 0.41 but retrieves only unrelated papers (MRR 0). The RAG engine
  translates it first (section 5); retrieval alone would fail.
- **Similarity gate:** all 4 off-domain questions are stopped before the LLM
  (best similarity 0.19-0.22 < 0.30). The 6 hard in-domain questions score
  0.32-0.51, overlapping the lowest answerable questions (0.52-0.53), so the
  gate cannot separate them and the LLM has to.

## 7. Weaknesses and proposed fixes

Ordered by impact. Effort is an _estimate_ (S = hours, M = a day or more).

| # | Weakness (evidence) | Proposed fix | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Fixed, see section 9.** **Answer summary is not validated** (`concept-drift` summary repeats rejected citations and shows raw "E3" markers; `vehicle-security` shows a summary under `insufficient_evidence`) | Show a summary only when every claim was verified, or rebuild it from verified claims; strip bare `E\d+` mentions; hide the summary for abstentions | S |
| 2 | **HNSW index not used** (planner picks a sequential scan at ef_search=200; HNSW 2.3 ms vs exact 19.8 ms, recall@40 0.984) | **Tried, measured and rejected for this corpus (2026-11-06):** ef_search=100 makes the planner use the index (retrieval query 39 ms -> 5 ms) but recall@40 falls to 0.946 and MRR from 0.858 to 0.824, because `insider-threat` loses its best chunk. `hnsw.iterative_scan` did not change recall, and rebuilding with m=32, ef_construction=400 only reached 0.981 at ef_search=100. The exact scan is kept and now set explicitly (`rag.exact_vector_search`), because the planner's choice flipped to the index after a schema change: 19 ms is negligible beside a 73 s answer. Turn it off only when the corpus is large enough to need it; at 1M chunks a linear scan would take roughly 2.6 s (_estimate_: 19.8 ms x 130) | - |
| 3 | **Improved, see section 9.** **Generic "hub" documents win retrieval** (`federated-ids` MRR 0.17, `ids-datasets` 0.14; a 500-page management book is the top hit; context precision only 52%) | Hybrid ranking: add the PostgreSQL full-text rank to the cosine score (the FTS ranker found the relevant papers); cap chunks per long book; measure a cross-encoder reranker with `evaluate --part retrieval` | M |
| 4 | **Corpus off-topic and narrow** (25.5% without any security term; 4 days of 2026 only; Ukrainian finance papers) | Tighten the OpenAlex query (require a security keyword in title/abstract, `language:en`), then run `backfill --start 2023-01-01` | S + run time |
| 5 | **Missing text** (48 papers = 12.7% with no PDF and no abstract; 15 of 101 judged-relevant papers are therefore unsearchable) | Fill abstracts from OpenAlex `abstract_inverted_index` / Semantic Scholar when another source has one | S |
| 6 | **Citation-valid but irrelevant or wrong claims** (Vietnamese answer cites a management book for an IDS claim) | Per-claim faithfulness check: LLM judge (Claude when a key exists) or an NLI model on (quote, claim); add human review of `answers.json` to the protocol | M |
| 7 | **False abstention** (`vehicle-security`: valid evidence, model said insufficient) | Prompt: "insufficient" only when no excerpt supports any part; turn >= 1 verified evidence claim into `partial` instead of hiding it; compare with Claude | S |
| 8 | **Slow local answers** (73 s mean, 106 s p95, 99.9% in the LLM) | Use the Claude API when a key exists (latency not yet measured); stream tokens to the terminal; cap output tokens | S |
| 9 | **Embedding is the processing bottleneck** (41.7 chunks/s vs 127.6 pages/s extraction) | Run Sentence Transformers on the GPU (CUDA PyTorch) or embed in parallel Spark partitions for backfills | S |
| 10 | Similarity gate cannot stop in-domain unanswerable questions (hard negatives 0.32-0.51 vs lowest answerable 0.52) | Keep the gate (it stops off-domain questions for free) and rely on the LLM, which declined 6/6; recalibrate on this dataset when the corpus grows | - |

## 8. What this evaluation does not show

- Answer *correctness* beyond citation checks: a claim can quote a real
  sentence and still overstate it. Completeness is a lexical proxy. A human
  review of `docs/evaluation/answers.json` (it stores every shown answer) or an
  LLM judge (Claude, when a key is available) would be the next step.
- The Claude API: latency, refusal behaviour and answer quality with
  `claude-opus-5-5` were not measured.
- Scale: 7,691 chunks fit in memory; latency at 1M chunks is an _estimate_
  only (section 7), not a measurement.
- Relevance labels are silver (assistant-judged, pooled); see section 2.

## 9. Quality fixes after the evaluation (measured 2026-11-07)

Sections 3-6 report the system as first evaluated; those results are kept in
`docs/evaluation/before_quality_fixes/`. The JSON files directly in
`docs/evaluation/` are now from the fixed system.

**Fixes:** (1) a summary is removed when any citation of the answer was rejected
or it contains raw evidence ids, and when nothing could be verified neither summary
nor interpretation is shown; raw ids in claims become reference numbers.
(2) Hybrid retrieval: the cosine ranking is merged by reciprocal rank fusion with a
full-text search over a stored `tsvector` column. (3) The vector search is set to
an exact scan explicitly (row 2 of section 7).

**Choosing the hybrid variant** (retrieval metrics, 29 English questions, `rrf_k`
60; "rare" = in at most 10% or 5% of the chunks):

| Full-text list | MRR | P@1 | P@5 | R@5 | R@10 | Evidence coverage | Context precision | Context paper recall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| None (before) | 0.858 | 0.793 | 0.352 | 0.756 | 0.821 | 0.931 | 0.522 | 0.752 |
| Any question term | 0.864 | 0.793 | 0.338 | 0.734 | 0.806 | 0.966 | 0.418 | 0.734 |
| Any rare term (10%) | 0.878 | 0.828 | 0.331 | 0.712 | 0.841 | 1.000 | 0.397 | 0.722 |
| Any rare term (5%) | 0.926 | 0.897 | 0.338 | 0.736 | 0.847 | 1.000 | 0.422 | 0.764 |
| **All rare terms (10%), chosen** | **0.908** | **0.862** | **0.379** | **0.789** | **0.833** | **1.000** | **0.534** | **0.789** |
| Title + abstract, papers | 0.852 | 0.759 | 0.345 | 0.728 | 0.854 | 1.000 | 0.392 | 0.810 |
| Chunks + title and abstract | 0.925 | 0.862 | 0.359 | 0.741 | 0.832 | 1.000 | 0.418 | 0.774 |

The chosen variant is the only one better than the baseline on all these metrics.
The 5% variant has a higher MRR but fills the context with more irrelevant chunks,
which is what the LLM reads. MRR per question: `federated-ids` 0.167 -> 1.0,
`prompt-injection` 0.5 -> 1.0, `ids-datasets` 0.143 -> 0.333, `edge-ids`
0.333 -> 0.25 (worse).

**Answer quality** (same 40 questions and model):

| Metric | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| Citation validity rate | 87.2% (82/94) | 87.8% (79/90) |
| Unsupported claim rate (model) | 9.7% (7/72) | 9.6% (7/73) |
| Unsupported claims shown to the user | 0 | 0 |
| Evidence relevance | 0.747 | 0.762 |
| Answer completeness (lexical proxy) | 0.525 | 0.606 |
| Answer rate, answerable questions | 93.3% (19 answered, 9 partial) | 96.7% (19 answered, 10 partial) |
| Correct abstention, unanswerable | 100% (10/10) | 100% (10/10) |
| Answers showing raw evidence ids | present (`concept-drift`) | 0 |
| Retrieval + validation time | 0.074 s | 0.092 s |
| LLM generation time (mean) | 73.3 s | 104.6 s |

- **Better:** `federated-ids` and `vi-federated-ids` now answer from relevant papers
  (the first abstained, the second cited a management book).
- **Worse:** `prompt-injection` went from answered to `insufficient_evidence` in this
  run, and `concept-drift` from answered to `partial`. `edge-ids`, `lm-threat-hunting`
  and `payment-fraud` lost completeness.
- **Model noise, measured.** The `prompt-injection` abstention did not reproduce: five
  more runs with the same 3,017-token prompt all gave `answered` with verified
  citations. So the local model can give a different answer to an identical prompt
  despite temperature 0, and one run per question cannot separate small differences
  from noise. How often this happens across the other questions was not measured.
- **LLM time is not comparable.** Output length is unchanged (426 vs 430 tokens). The
  cause of the slower run was not investigated; other load on the machine during the
  run was not ruled out.

**Limits of these numbers.** The hybrid variant and its thresholds were chosen on the
same 29 questions they are reported on, so the gain may be optimistic. Labels are
silver and from a pool built with dense and full-text search. Ordering changes also
change which excerpts the local model sees, so single-question changes in answers
include model noise and should not be read as caused by one fix.

## 10. Big Data pipeline (measured 2026-11-07)

Everything below ran on one machine, in Docker: one NameNode and two DataNodes with
replication 2, one Kafka broker, and Spark in `local[*]` mode. It shows the mechanisms
on real data, not cluster speed or protection against a machine failing.

**Data lake on HDFS** from the Kaggle arXiv snapshot (5.67 GB of JSON,
[raw report](bigdata/lake_report.json)):

| Layer | Records | Stored | Seconds |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bronze (all raw records) | 3,233,620 (0 corrupt) | 2,732 MB (5,464 MB with replicas) | 83.3 |
| Silver (`cs.CR`, cleaned) | 53,943 | 52.8 MB | 31.6 |
| Gold (statistics) | - | under 0.1 MB | 8.5 |

Silver rejected 7 withdrawn papers and 26 duplicate titles. The 53,976 records with
`cs.CR` equal an independent line count of the raw file. The de-duplication matches
exact normalized titles only, so differently worded re-posts remain.

**Python vs Spark** on the same task, raw JSON to the Silver result: plain Python took
249.2 s and Spark 88.5 s, about 2.8 times faster, with identical counts. This is one
run each. Spark ran first, so Python may have benefited from the file being cached,
and the gain comes from using several CPU cores, not from several machines.

**A stall found and fixed.** The first full run hung for about three hours with no
tasks running, in a stage that mixed shuffle and HDFS tasks. With the default
`spark.locality.wait` the same write was still stuck when stopped after 240 s; with 0
it finished in 16.7 s. The likely cause is that local mode does not offer delayed
tasks again. The Spark source was not read to confirm this.

**Streaming** (crawler, Kafka, Spark Structured Streaming, PostgreSQL), live with arXiv
and OpenAlex into a separate database:

- A micro-batch started at most 17.2 s after its oldest message was sent while the
  consumer was waiting (trigger every 30 s). This is one small batch of 14 messages,
  and it does not include embedding.
- One malformed message was set aside on HDFS with its reason; the stream continued.
- Hard kill (`docker kill`) in the middle of a batch: 162 papers were stored, none of
  them newly embedded. After a restart the batch was replayed: all 269 messages were
  handled, ending with 250 sources from 250 distinct ids, no duplicate chunks and no
  paper with an abstract missing its chunks.
- Replaying the whole topic of 18 messages from the start after deleting the
  checkpoint changed no counts in the database.
- The evaluated corpus stayed at 377 papers throughout.

Not measured: throughput under sustained load, or a Kafka broker failure. The numbers
in sections 2-9 do not include streamed papers.

Read these with care. The first result is right less often, but the top 5 hold more relevant
papers. Precision@5 rises and recall@5 falls largely because each question now has many more
relevant papers, not because the search changed. Exact scan and HNSW gave the same scores
here. The two columns use different label sets and are not a controlled comparison.

Limits of the new labels: one AI judge (Claude), from the title and the first 32 words of the
abstract, with no human check. The judge saw the question and the pool, and the same system built
most of the pool, so relevant papers it never found stay unknown and recall is optimistic. The
thresholds were chosen on the small corpus, not tuned here.

ChatGPT (model version not recorded) judged a blind sample of 56 papers from 6 questions, reading the full abstracts.
The two judges agreed on 52 of 56 (92.9%, Cohen's kappa 0.857). ChatGPT found relevant 3
of the 30 papers Claude had rejected and rejected 1 of the 26 it had accepted. This is agreement
between two AI judges, not human validation, and they may share the same mistakes. The sample was
chosen to balance both labels across 6 questions, so the rate does not describe all 681 judged
papers. In two disagreements (both on `gnn-ids`) the use of graph neural networks appears only
later in the abstract, past what Claude read, so some relevant papers may be labelled as not
relevant. The labels were left unchanged.

The unanswerable set also changed: `hard-bgp` and `hard-wpa3` became answerable, because this
corpus has direct evidence for them. The similarity gate catches 3 of the remaining 8 questions.
All 8 were abstained on in the answer run below.

The citation checks held on the larger corpus and answers cite more papers, but they lean heavily
on abstracts. `hard-bgp` and `hard-wpa3`, unanswerable before, now get partial answers.

Key-point coverage fell on 17 questions and rose on 5, and this result cannot be read as a plain
loss of quality. The key points were written from the 377 papers, while 81 of the 99 cited papers
are new, so an answer built on different valid papers can score low. The ddos answer scored 0.0
because it covers classical models and metrics rather than the listed recurrent, flash-crowd and
federated points. It may also reflect a real loss of detail from abstract-only evidence, and this
run cannot separate the two. Measuring it would need key points rewritten for the larger corpus.
